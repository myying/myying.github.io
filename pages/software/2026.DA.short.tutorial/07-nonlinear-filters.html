Nonlinear filters

Chapters 1–6 built every analysis scheme on two linear-Gaussian assumptions: the observation operator is (locally) linear and the prior error is Gaussian. Both fail for coherent structures like tropical cyclones. This chapter starts with the most common failure mode — position error — and will build up to particle filters, which replace the Gaussian assumption altogether.

1 · Position errors break the Gaussian assumption

A Rankine vortex — solid-body rotation inside the radius of maximum wind, a (Rmw/r)1.5 decay outside, the profile used in Ying 2019 — is a minimal model of a coherent vortex. The ensemble below has 100 members that differ only in where the vortex sits: each centre is drawn from a Gaussian with standard deviation Lsprd around the truth location (given in units of the radius of maximum wind Rmw). The observation point P (marked +) sits southeast of the truth centre (compass 135°), just outside the radius of maximum wind, and the state we watch is the zonal wind u at P (a strong westerly of about 23 m/s there). Click P on panel (a) to move it anywhere. Slide Lsprd up and down: at small spread the error u − utruth at P is Gaussian; once the spread reaches the vortex size the histogram skews and fattens, and no Gaussian prior can represent it.

Why the bend. The wind at a fixed point is the tangential profile evaluated at the member centre's distance from that point. Near P that map is locally linear, so a small position spread keeps the error at P Gaussian. As the spread grows, members' centres pass P: the wind there peaks as a member's core edge reaches P and then reverses sign as the centre passes P (panel (c)) — a non-monotone map pushed through a Gaussian cloud of centres. The histogram in panel (b) skews and fattens, and the readout's excess kurtosis leaves 0. An ensemble that is Gaussian in where the storm is is emphatically non-Gaussian in what the wind is at any fixed point.

This widget is the interactive version of the figure in Ying 2019, “multiscale alignment” — the same Rankine vortex ensemble, the same conclusion: as the location spread increases, the prior error distribution deviates more from a Gaussian, and the linear-Gaussian EnKF analysis becomes suboptimal. The displacement-error routine used here is the one from the accompanying rankine code.

2 · EnKF vs particle filter on the same prior

When the Gaussian assumptions fail, Bayes' rule is solved directly on the ensemble: the particle filter turns the prior members into a weighted sample, each particle reweighted by the observation likelihood p(y | centre). The EnKF cannot do that — its analysis is an affine combination of the prior members, so the posterior keeps the Gaussian shape of the prior. The widget below runs both filters on the same vortex ensemble from section 1, after assimilating a single observation of the zonal wind u at P (the +): the prior (blue), the EnKF analysis (red), and the particle-filter posterior (green, dot size = weight).

Try it: drag y away from the truth value (the default is already a 3σo outlier): the EnKF posterior is pulled only partway and stays a broad Gaussian, while the particle filter reweights the particles toward the observation — and watch the effective sample size Neff collapse. Slide Lsprd up to make the prior u at P non-Gaussian, and Nens to change how many particles the filters share.

Reading the panels. (a) The three ensembles in position space: the prior cloud (blue), the EnKF analysis (red) — still a Gaussian blob, just shifted and shrunk along the regression direction — and the particle-filter posterior (green), which reweights the same prior particles, so its support is wherever the likelihood allows the vortex to be. (b) The three distributions of the observed quantity u at P: the prior can be skewed (once Lsprd reaches the vortex size), the EnKF posterior is always near-Gaussian and lags the observation, the PF posterior (weighted histogram) hugs the observation. (c) Why: u at P as a function of the centre displacement is non-monotone, so the observation line y crosses it at several displacements — the likelihood is multimodal in position space, and only the particle filter can carry that structure. The readout's effective sample size Neff = 1/Σwm2 quantifies the price: when the observation is unlikely under the prior, the weights collapse onto a few particles.
