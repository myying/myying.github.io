Nonlinear filters

Chapters 1–6 built every analysis scheme on two linear-Gaussian assumptions: the observation operator is (locally) linear and the prior error is Gaussian. Both fail for coherent structures like tropical cyclones. This chapter starts with the most common failure mode — position error — and will build up to particle filters, which replace the Gaussian assumption altogether.

1 · Position errors break the Gaussian assumption

A Rankine vortex — solid-body rotation inside the radius of maximum wind, a (Rmw/r)1.5 decay outside, the profile used in Ying 2019 — is a minimal model of a coherent vortex. The ensemble below has 400 members that differ only in where the vortex sits: each centre is drawn from a Gaussian with standard deviation Lsprd around the truth location. The observation point P (marked +) is the truth centre, where the displacement error shows up most clearly: members whose vortex has passed P produce winds of either sign, so the wind error there is the classic “storm-here-or-there” distribution. Slide Lsprd up and down: at small spread the error at P is Gaussian; once the spread reaches the vortex size it flattens and fattens, and no Gaussian prior can represent it.

Near the centre the wind at P grows linearly with the displacement, so a small spread keeps the error Gaussian; once members' centres reach the core edge (r = Rmw, dashed) — or stray off the P-axis — the response bends and the error distribution of panel (b) leaves Gaussian.

Why the S-curve. At a fixed point, the wind is the tangential profile evaluated at the member centre's distance from that point. Near the truth centre that map is linear — so small position spreads keep the error Gaussian. But once members' centres wander past the radius of maximum wind, or off the axis to the point, the response bends (panel (c)): the histogram in panel (b) flattens into the “storm here or there” shape and the readout's excess kurtosis leaves 0. An ensemble that is Gaussian in where the storm is is emphatically non-Gaussian in what the wind is at any fixed point.

This widget is the interactive version of the figure in Ying 2019, “multiscale alignment” — the same Rankine vortex ensemble, the same conclusion: as the location spread increases, the prior error distribution deviates more from a Gaussian, and the linear-Gaussian EnKF analysis becomes suboptimal. 2 · Particle filters

When the Gaussian assumptions fail, Bayes' rule is solved directly on a set of weighted samples — particle filters. A planned widget will show weight collapse: the particles pile onto a single trajectory as the filter struggles with a strongly nonlinear observation, and why resampling fights it.
