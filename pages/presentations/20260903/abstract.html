I gave a keynote presentation at the 8th WMO Symposium on Data Assimilation (jointly held with the 12th International Symposium on Data Assimilation) at Nanjing University, China, in the "DA for the Earth System" session, titled "Advancing multiscale and non-Gaussian data assimilation methodology for Earth system prediction problems".

Earth-system prediction problems push data assimilation (DA) methods to their limits: high dimensionality for resolving multiscale physical processes demands DA methods to be computationally efficient, while high nonlinearity from chaotic dynamics requires methods to handle non-Gaussian error distributions. The Ensemble Kalman Filter remains the workhorse for large-scale systems due to its scalability, but its linear-Gaussian assumption breaks down under nonlinearity, motivating a push toward scalable non-Gaussian methods, including AI-augmented DA. In this talk, I presented an evolution of my multiscale alignment technique, which augments the alignment step with an AI-based optical flow algorithm (Dense Inverse Search). The new approach showed improved skill over existing multiscale and ensemble filter baselines in an idealized hurricane model. This work is enabled by NEDAS, a Python-based testbed built for rapid development and benchmarking of ensemble and non-Gaussian DA methods, with a modular and scalable architecture that makes it straightforward to plug in new geophysical models and to draw on the Python ecosystem for AI-DA integration.
