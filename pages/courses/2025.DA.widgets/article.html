Observation–State Correlation Explorer

A single temperature observation (red circle) is assimilated from a smooth 2-D field. The ensemble gives an estimate of how that observation correlates with the temperature state at every grid point — the core quantity of covariance-based data assimilation, reduced to a single variable so the idea is unmistakable. The numbered annotations below explain what each panel shows.

Try it: click or drag anywhere on a map to move the black state marker — the correlation map and the scatter respond live.

Reading the panels

1 · The truth field — the “answer key” we are trying to estimate from sparse observations: a single warm blob (peak ≈ 7.9 K, radius scale 100 km) on a 50×50 grid with 10 km cells. The black square marks the state location (i, j) you are inspecting.

2 · The correlation map — how the observation covaries with the state at every grid point, estimated from the 100-member ensemble. The ensemble perturbs only the blob’s position (std 50 km), so the pattern is a clean dipole: +0.98 right at the observation, −0.64 on the far side, fading with distance. Position uncertainty is the classic mechanism that produces strong negative correlation.

3 · The scatter — the joint distribution of the observation ensemble (x) and the state ensemble at the marker (y). Each dot is one member; the amber line is the observed value, the ink line the truth. Move the marker across the dipole and watch the slope — and the reported r — flip sign.

Key idea: correlation is what the analysis uses

Every covariance-based assimilation method (EnKF, 3D-Var, …) needs the same quantity this widget visualises: the correlation between each observation and each state variable. The ensemble estimate, computed with the same ens_corr formula as vort2d/diagnostics.py in the NEDAS tutorials, is

\[ r = \frac{\sum_{m=1}^{M} (x_m - \bar{x})(y_m - \bar{y})} {\sqrt{\sum_{m=1}^{M} (x_m - \bar{x})^2}\;\sqrt{\sum_{m=1}^{M} (y_m - \bar{y})^2}}, \]

with \(x_m\) the observation-prior ensemble and \(y_m\) the state ensemble at the marker. In the analysis step the increment at a grid point is proportional to its correlation with the observation times the innovation (observed − prior),

\[ \Delta T(i,j) \;\propto\; r(i,j)\cdot\big(y^{o} - \bar{y}\big). \]

Points on the positive lobe warm up when the observation is warm and points on the negative lobe cool down — the dipole tells the analysis to move the warm blob toward the observation rather than merely smear the warmth around it. That is the microscopic picture behind the correlation maps used throughout the NEDAS tutorials.
