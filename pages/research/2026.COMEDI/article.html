COpernicus Marine services with Enhanced Data assimilation and improved Interoperability

COMEDI will modernise and harmonise data assimilation (DA) practices across all Monitoring and Forecasting Centres (MFCs) of the Copernicus Marine Service, enabling them to leverage new technologies and use new types of ocean observations to achieve better accuracy and reliability.

Modernise and harmonise

MFCs rely on simplified DA methods, and each develops its own DA software independently. COMEDI brings research advances into operations and builds shared tools so the centres can exchange them.

1

SO-1Advance DA methods

Advance the DA methods in current MFC operations by implementing novel techniques for uncertainty representation and assimilation, integrating AI, to achieve better accuracy and reliability of the operational products.

Today in the MFCs → with COMEDI

EnsemblesMostly static covariances; only ARC and GLO run dynamic ensemblesDynamic ensembles in EnKFs, blended into 3DVar
Non-GaussianAll operational methods assume Gaussian errorsThree non-Gaussian filters for coupled BGC and sea-ice DA
AIPrototypes exist but are not used in operationsAI bias correction, super-resolution and generative AI as a core DA method

Key results

  • 2 multiscale methods for uncertainty representation
  • 1 perturbation scheme for coupled physics–BGC models
  • 2 hybrid climatological–dynamical covariance techniques
  • 3 AI methods for systematic errors, subgrid uncertainty and ensemble diversity
  • 3 non-Gaussian ensemble filters
  • 2 generative AI algorithms used as core DA

SO-2Make best use of observations

Prepare the MFCs and users to take maximum benefit from novel ocean observations, upgrading the DA methods to make better use of observation information and implementing diagnostic tools to evaluate their impact systematically.

Today in the MFCs → with COMEDI

TopicTodayCOMEDI
Sea iceRetrieved sea-ice and surface temperature productsDirect assimilation of brightness temperatures in coupled ocean–sea ice DA
High resolutionObservation errors treated as uncorrelated, except along-track altimetryCorrelated observation errors in both 3DVar and EnKFs
New missionsPrototype operators for S3-NG, CIMR and reflectanceS3-NG wide-swath altimetry, CIMR brightness temperatures and reflectance assimilated in real MFC settings
ImpactData-denial experiments; cross-MFC evaluation is hardAssessment protocol, systematic OSSEs and OSEs, and FSOI for 3DVar and EnKFs

Key results

  • 1 updated observation operator for the CIMR mission
  • 1 method for correlated observation errors in wide-swath altimetry
  • 2 tools for forecast sensitivity to observation impact (FSOI)
  • An AI emulator of FSOI at least 10× faster, in the GLO system
  • 2 OSSEs: S3-NG in the NWS MFC and CIMR in the ARC MFC
  • OSEs for reflectance data in 3 MFCs

SO-3Improve interoperability

Improve the interoperability of the MFCs by developing modular software components and shared interfaces, enabling a more efficient exchange of new methods among operational centres. Each MFC keeps its established workflow while connecting to common DA core methods.

Today in the MFCs → with COMEDI

TopicTodayCOMEDI
Method exchangeLimited, because the MFCs share no DA softwareInteroperable tools with unit tests, and interfaces to common DA software
TeamsDA developed independently at each MFCThree Joint Development Teams: AI, Protocol and Software

Key results

  • 7 developments from WP2–5 implemented on at least two MFC DA platforms
  • 3 interfaces to a common assimilation core (PDAF), linking 4 ensemble-based MFCs
  • 2 variational platforms sharing the same NEMO observation operator
  • 5 DA software packages interfaced to AI models
  • The STOPACK perturbation package available for both NEMO and HYCOM

SO-4Prepare innovations for operational use

Prepare DA method innovations for operational use by demonstrating them in realistic MFC configurations, reaching Technology Readiness Level 6, and by engaging CMEMS stakeholders for feedback.

Key results

  • 7 demonstrations for physical ocean products with 10% error reduction (GLO ×2, BAL, BLK, MED, NWS, IBI)
  • 1 demonstration for coupled physics–sea ice products (ARC)
  • 5 demonstrations for coupled physics–BGC products (GLO, ARC, BAL, BLK, MED)
  • 2 user workshops with 30 participants each
  • A roadmap for transferring the results into CMEMS operations

More about the project

Project details

Copernicus Marine Service

From observations to users

Every MFC production system has the same building blocks. Data assimilation combines model predictions with observations to find the best estimate of the ocean state, which then starts the forecasts and reanalyses that users rely on.

Observe

Satellites and in situ networks such as Argo floats measure sea level, temperature, salinity, sea ice and ocean colour.

Assimilate and forecast COMEDI

The MFCs combine the observations with ocean, biogeochemistry and sea-ice models through data assimilation.

Deliver products

Copernicus Marine publishes near-real-time forecasts and multi-year reanalyses, with their uncertainties.

Serve users

~30,000 active users, over 100,000 registered

  • Shipping and navigation
  • Fisheries and aquaculture
  • Offshore industries
  • Coastal services
  • Policy makers
  • Environmental NGOs
  • Academia
  • The general public

Where it applies

The seven Monitoring and Forecasting Centres

Each MFC produces the ocean analyses and forecasts for one region and is represented by at least one COMEDI partner. Most run the NEMO ocean model; the Arctic MFC uses HYCOM. Their DA software differs from centre to centre.
