Nonlinear filters

Chapters 1–6 built every analysis scheme on two linear-Gaussian assumptions: the observation operator is (locally) linear and the prior error is Gaussian. Both fail for coherent structures like tropical cyclones. This chapter starts with the most common failure mode — position error — and will build up to particle filters, which replace the Gaussian assumption altogether.

1 · Position errors break the Gaussian assumption

A Rankine vortex — solid-body rotation inside the radius of maximum wind, a (Rmw/r)1.5 decay outside, the profile used in Ying 2019 — is a minimal model of a coherent vortex. The ensemble below has 400 members that differ only in where the vortex sits: each centre is drawn from a Gaussian with standard deviation Lsprd around the truth location. The observation point P (marked +) sits southeast of the truth centre (compass 135°), just outside the radius of maximum wind, and the state we watch is the zonal wind u at P (a strong westerly there, about 23 m/s short of calm). Slide Lsprd up and down: at small spread the error u − utruth at P is Gaussian; once the spread reaches the vortex size the histogram skews and fattens, and no Gaussian prior can represent it.

A small spread rides the local slope and keeps the error Gaussian; a wide spread straddles the bend, and the error distribution of panel (b) skews and fattens.

Why the bend. The wind at a fixed point is the tangential profile evaluated at the member centre's distance from that point. Near P that map is locally linear, so a small position spread keeps the error at P Gaussian. As the spread grows, members' centres pass P: the wind there peaks as a member's core edge reaches P and then reverses sign as the centre passes P (panel (c)) — a non-monotone map pushed through a Gaussian cloud of centres. The histogram in panel (b) skews and fattens, and the readout's excess kurtosis leaves 0. An ensemble that is Gaussian in where the storm is is emphatically non-Gaussian in what the wind is at any fixed point.

This widget is the interactive version of the figure in Ying 2019, “multiscale alignment” — the same Rankine vortex ensemble, the same conclusion: as the location spread increases, the prior error distribution deviates more from a Gaussian, and the linear-Gaussian EnKF analysis becomes suboptimal. 2 · Particle filters

When the Gaussian assumptions fail, Bayes' rule is solved directly on a set of weighted samples — particle filters. A planned widget will show weight collapse: the particles pile onto a single trajectory as the filter struggles with a strongly nonlinear observation, and why resampling fights it.
